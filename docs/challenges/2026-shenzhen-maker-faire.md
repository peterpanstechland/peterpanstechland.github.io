# 2026 Shenzhen Maker Faire · MAKERS CHALLENGE

## BEG. BUILD. SHOW. — the booth-to-demo maker experiment

**No fixed BOM. No finished robot in your backpack. Discover the components, meet the makers, build a weird functional prototype, and demo it live.**

:::caution PROPOSAL — NOT AN OFFICIAL EVENT ANNOUNCEMENT
**Peter Pan's Techland is proposing this independent community activity.** Maker Faire Shenzhen 2026 is publicly scheduled for **December 12–13, 2026, Nanshan District, Shenzhen**, but the challenge itself, organizer authorization, challenge venue, participation, schedule, sponsors, and awards are **not confirmed**. The official event website is [Maker Faire Shenzhen](https://www.shenzhenmakerfaire.com/). This page describes a proposed format open for discussion.
:::

## 01 / The big idea

Maker Faire is full of builders, products, ideas, and idle demo boards. What happens if a team starts with **almost nothing** and creates a new device by visiting participating booths?

- **For participants:** improvise under constraints, practice talking to other makers, and ship a real demo.
- **For exhibitors:** see your product appear in an unexpected working build, plus verified credits and documentation.
- **For attendees:** watch the real creative process—not just a display case or presentation.
- **For the community:** connect teams, exhibitors and independent makers through a visible contribution trail.

**Tagline:** **BEG. BUILD. SHOW.** / **MAKE SOMETHING RIDICULOUS.** “Beg” is playful maker slang; contributions are always voluntary and respectful.

## 03 / Who can join and what to bring

**Proposed:** teams of 2–4, including independent makers, hardware developers, students, designers, artists, engineers, educators and curious builders. Mixed-discipline teams are encouraged. Solo applicants may request team matching. Minors need event-organizer approval, guardian consent and supervised safe tool use.

**Bring:** personal laptop, IDEs, reference material, normal debugging software, safety glasses and reasonable personal hand tools. Permitted pre-existing knowledge, libraries, open-source code, SDKs and development environments do not count as a pre-built solution.

**Do not bring:** a functioning core competition device; a preassembled hardware kit designed for your planned demo; undeclared premium components to bypass sourcing; dangerous equipment or prohibited substances. Teams may propose access accommodations; organizers should ensure comparable support.

## 04 / Maker Passport & the booth economy

Every team gets a numbered **Maker Passport** (physical card + optional QR record). Every voluntarily participating exhibitor gets a simple **CONTRIBUTOR** marker and knows what it is willing to offer.

| Contribution type | Ownership / control | How it is recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GIFT** — product, module, component, material, consumable | Donated to the team only with explicit agreement | Booth name, description, quantity, reference value if supplied, receiver, timestamp |
| **LOAN** — test gear, reusable module, camera, robot hand | Remains the owner's property; return by logged deadline | Asset ID / condition, approved use, responsible borrower, checkout / return |
| **TOOL ACCESS** — shared soldering, 3D printer, oscilloscope | Supervised use only; never transferred | Slot, operator, hours / material use |
| **MENTOR HELP** — debugging, SDKs, technical explanation | Advice / assistance, not goods | Mentor name, booth, type of help |
| **EXCHANGE** — team-to-team part swap | Only if original donor terms permit | Both teams, transfer reason, updated provenance |

**Suggested sourcing mechanics (subject to final materials supply):**

1. Booth participation and donation are **opt-in**, and a booth can say no at any time. Suggested limit: **at most one donated unit per team per visit**; exhibitor may set stricter quantities or distribute via a common parts pool.
2. Teams should aim for **five distinct exhibitor interactions** and integrate tangible contributions from **at least three distinct exhibitors** into the final demo. Organizers may lower or waive thresholds when stock or accessibility does not support them; no team should be penalized for donors' refusals.
3. A **shared baseline essentials pool** (breadboard, standard connectors, basic controller if available, fasteners, wire, safe power) prevents the entire challenge from becoming a lottery of who found the most expensive board. All use must be logged.
4. Premium equipment and scarce modules should be handled through a **common checkout / reservation queue**, not by private exclusive arrangements. Fairness overrides sticker price; gift value does not score points.
5. Each contribution can be registered on the passport through a stamped card, handwritten record or QR form. The final story map credits the contributor with their consent.
6. Optional **Mystery Object**: every team receives one equally distributed silly low-risk item, e.g. a rubber duck, cardboard object or old phone shell, and must incorporate it meaningfully. Only activate if supplies and safety are confirmed.

### What makes a great donation?

A useful gift is **small, safe, documented, and available in quantities**: a sensor breakout, LED ring, motor driver, ESP32 board, cable bundle, display, 3D-printed joint, enclosure offcut, switch or button. Exhibitors are never required to donate display samples or costly inventory.

## 05 / The three challenge phases

### Phase 01 — BEG / Meet + Collect

At the briefing, teams receive the safety rules, the common-pool policy and passport. Explore only the approved exhibitor area; explain your intent, obtain permission before picking up anything, ask for a component or technical advice, record every transfer, and tell the booth owner what you hope to build. No aggressive solicitation, crowd blocking, misleading sponsorship claims or dismantling booth displays.

### Phase 02 — BUILD / Remix + Test

Return to the common build zone, inventory parts and choose a feasible demo. Sketch an architecture with power/safety constraints, prototype in small increments, book shared tools, and test integration early. Teams can ask mentors and use AI code assistants; however, their team must perform the key hardware integration, understand the implementation and disclose substantial third-party help.

### Phase 03 — SHOW / 3-Minute Live Demo

Each team gets **3 minutes to demonstrate + 2 minutes for judges' questions** (proposal; final timing depends on stage availability). Show the device operating, say which booths made it possible, explain the engineering trade-offs and be truthful about limitations. A video may document a previous working state but **cannot substitute for the required safe live demonstration** for the main award. Minor failures are judged transparently; do not hide them.

## 07 / Judging and awards

Safety is a **pass/fail gate**, not a score bonus. The judges may stop unsafe demonstrations immediately; teams receive a reasonable chance to fix low-risk issues before the scheduled demo cut-off.

| Criterion | Weight | What judges look for |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Working live demo | **30** | Functional core interaction, clear proof, reproducible operation |
| Creativity / surprise | **20** | Imaginative use, original concept, playful experience |
| Cross-booth collaboration | **20** | Meaningful integration, accurate passport credits, respectful collaboration |
| Engineering & resilience | **15** | Safe circuits, sensible architecture, debugging, robustness |
| Demo storytelling | **10** | Clarity, audience engagement, acknowledgement of support |
| Resourceful / reusable design | **5** | Sensible use of offcuts, repairs, minimal waste |
| **Total** | **100** | Ties: higher live-demo score, then judge consensus |

**Suggested award categories (subject to award sponsorship):** **FrankenMaker Award** (best cross-booth monster), **Most Useless Useful Invention** (delightfully ridiculous), **Hardcore Maker Award** (engineering), **Cyber Beggar / Networking Master** (collaboration), and **People's Choice** (attendee voting). A participation certificate or sticker can replace cash prizes; **no prize pool is guaranteed at this stage**. Exhibitors and monetary sponsors may not purchase votes, judgeships, or scoring advantages.

## 08 / Shared workbench, devices and safety

**Needed zones:** check-in / passports, contribution registry, shared hardware pool, build tables, controlled soldering/tool area, supervised 3D printing, charging and safe power, a small stage or demo desk, fire egress and waste/recycling area. Appoint named on-duty safety and equipment custodians.

**Baseline safety policy proposed for venue review:**

- Use protected, low-voltage DC electronics by default (ideally **5–24 V**) with fuses/current limits and checked wiring. No participant-built mains/220 V circuitry on the public workbench. Venue-managed electrical equipment must be installed and inspected by qualified staff.
- No exposed mains wiring, open flame, pyrotechnics, pressurized projectiles, hazardous chemicals, weapons, or Class 3B/4 lasers. Restrict high-powered lasers, high-energy motors, fast-spinning mechanisms and autonomous moving systems until specifically assessed and contained.
- Li-ion/LiPo batteries require protected, intact packs, proper chargers, supervision and appropriate fire response procedures. Damaged / swollen batteries are not permitted.
- Soldering, cutting and 3D printers operate only in approved, ventilated areas under tool-steward supervision. Wear eye protection; children require guardian supervision and age-appropriate access.
- No camera/microphone capture of private data without consent. Use wireless devices within approved frequency and venue rules. No unapproved network scanning or interference.
- Equipment check-in/out records include condition photos where appropriate, usage boundaries, custody, emergency contacts and agreed damage terms. **Insurance, liability, deposits and replacement arrangements must be established by organizers and contributors before loans begin**, not improvised on-site.
- Anyone may call **STOP** for an immediate safety concern. Safety stewards can suspend a demo or disconnect power. Emergency evacuation follows venue staff instructions.

## 10 / What teams must submit

1. **Team / project card:** a name, one-sentence premise, and contact person.
2. **Maker Passport:** contributor booths, donated/borrowed components, mentor assistance and return records.
3. **Proof of building:** a working (safe) live demo and a simple circuit / system sketch; disclose any known limitations.
4. **3-minute story:** the concept, integrations, a surprise or debugging insight, and thanks to supporting exhibitors.
5. **Documentation:** 2–5 images or a 30–90 second video, permission-cleared sponsor credits, and a short parts list. Open-sourcing source code is encouraged but **not mandatory**; third-party IP and contributor rights remain intact.

## 14 / FAQ

**Must booths donate something?** No. Every gift, loan and mentoring interaction requires consent. No one may pressure a booth or remove display items.

**Can a team purchase extra parts?** To preserve the premise, direct competitive purchasing is prohibited once the build starts. Documented organizer-provided necessities and accessibility accommodations are exceptions.

**Can we bring laptops and existing code?** Yes. Standard programming tools, libraries and SDKs are allowed. A ready-made integrated demo brought in from outside is not.

**Can we use AI tools?** Yes. Teams must understand and responsibly integrate the result and disclose major outside assistance.

**What if some teams can't get enough parts?** The common pool, shared reservations, fair allocation and a possible lowered minimum booth threshold are there specifically to prevent this.

**Who owns the finished prototype?** Teams keep their original work and properly donated items, subject to pre-agreed contributor/IP terms. **Loaned assets must be returned**. Do not promise ownership of untransferred sponsor property.

**Are there real registrations or prize money already?** No. This page is a community proposal seeking participant interest and partnerships, not a confirmed ticket, sponsor list or cash prize announcement.
